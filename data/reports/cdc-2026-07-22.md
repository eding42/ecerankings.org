# Collection report: cdc (IEEE Conference on Decision and Control) — 2026-07-22

## Summary

Resolved the contradiction flagged in the task: the registry's `expected_per_year`
range of `[1100, 1800]` was **miscalibrated**, not evidence of undercounting.
Every one of the 10 previously-recorded years (2016–2025) was re-verified by
deep cursor-paging Crossref (not trusting the truncated `total-results` field,
which is inflated by fuzzy `query.container-title` matching) and DOI-prefix
filtering; two spot-checked years (2016, 2019) reproduced the registry's exact
counts (1216, 1203) bit-for-bit. Real single-edition CDC volume is consistently
**788–1216 papers/year**, with 2020 (788) a confirmed pandemic-year dip (fully
virtual conference) — nowhere close to the stated 1100 floor, let alone 1800.
The 1800-ish figure most likely leaked from an OpenAlex per-year source's
cumulative `works_count` field spanning multiple editions (e.g. the "40th CDC"
source reports 1850 total works across 2001–2003, not per year), rather than
from any single edition's real volume. No second co-located track or
uncaptured DOI prefix was found — the only lookalike is the unrelated Chinese
Control And Decision Conference (CCDC), whose DOI segments happen to contain
the substring "cdc" but whose container title ("Chinese Control and Decision
Conference") does not match "...Decision and Control", and which was
confirmed excluded from all counts. **Proposing `expected_per_year: [750,
1300]`** — the floor is set below the confirmed 2020 pandemic-year low (788)
so the range actually bounds every observed value, with headroom above the
historical high (1216). Year window checked: 2016–2026 (2017–2026 required,
plus the already-recorded 2016 kept per the skill's backfill allowance). 2026
is confirmed empty in Crossref — expected, since CDC 2026 (Honolulu, Dec
15–18, 2026) has not yet occurred; the skill's "include current year only if
already published" clause means this doesn't block verification. Recommending
**status: verified**, overriding the prior note's "candidate pending Phase
1b" rationale — that rationale predates this skill's updated "what verified
means" clarification (a DOI-validated Crossref year is legitimate coverage,
conference ingestion lag alone doesn't block verified); under the current
definition every check (identity, year coverage, volume under the corrected
range, 94.3%/93.9% affiliation coverage on the two OpenAlex-native years, and
the institution sniff test) passes. The original note's **volume-balance /
tier-2 concern is carried forward, not resolved** — that's a presentation
question for the area's methodology (Control = TAC + Automatica + CDC), not
a data-verification one, and is out of this task's scope.

## Volume-sanity resolution (the flagged contradiction)

Confirmed real, not a data problem. All 10 recorded years (2016–2025):
788, 1030, 1062, 1203, 936, 982, 1121, 1109, 1015, 1216 — range **788–1216**,
median ~1046. Only 3 of 10 (2016, 2019, 2023) cleared the old 1100 floor;
7 of 10 sat below it, and none came close to the 1800 ceiling. Likely origin
of the bad range: `works_count` on the two oldest per-year OpenAlex sources
(S4363608751 "40th CDC" = 1850, aggregating 2001–2003; S4363608267 "43rd
CDC, 2004" = 1042) — the 1850 figure is a plausible source for the phantom
1800 ceiling if someone read a multi-year cumulative field as a single-year
figure. **Proposed corrected `expected_per_year`: `[750, 1300]`** — bounds the
full observed 788–1216 (floor set below the confirmed 2020 low so the range
is self-consistent, not just "close"), while still being tight enough
to catch a genuinely wrong source (e.g. an accidental 2x merge) in future
checks.

A small unrelated catch-all source, `S4306418043` ("Conference on Decision
and Control", 240 works total, mostly pre-2015 with tiny in-window
remainders: 2016:4, 2017:4, 2018:8, 2019:7, 2021:2), was found but **not**
added to `openalex_ids` — its in-window counts are negligible and its 2021
entries likely overlap with works already counted under the native
S4363607724 source; adding it risks double-counting rather than adding real
coverage.
