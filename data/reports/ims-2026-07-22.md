### Corrected per-year sources (DOI-prefix validated today)

The registry's `doi_prefix` values for several years did not match any real
DOI pattern in Crossref. I re-derived every year's true prefix by pulling
~200–1000 Crossref records per year (query on container-title +
`type:proceedings-article` + date range) and tabulating the DOI's second
path segment, then confirming the top candidate's `container-title` reads
as the actual IMS proceedings (not a lookalike — IMS shares its publication
window with many MTT-S companion events: IWS, IMBioC, IMARC, WAMS, IMOC,
LAMC, AMS, RFM, IMFW, IMWS-AMP, IC-MAM, all excluded as separate
conferences, not IMS).

The `doi_prefix` field is what `pipeline/harvest_crossref.py` actually
filters on, so the 2016–2019/2022/2025 corrections are the load-bearing fix
here — without them, a Crossref harvest for those years would find zero (or
wrong) records. The small count deltas (~3–10% below previously-recorded
figures) are expected: Crossref keeps backfilling records after initial
collection, and my recount used a stricter DOI-prefix match rather than
trusting Crossref's (badly inflated, known-fuzzy) `total-results` field —
see Method note below. One caveat: my stopping heuristic (stop once the
target prefix's count stops growing across 2–3 pages) could very slightly
undercount a year where IMS isn't the top relevance-ranked container (true
for 2024, where IWS outranks it) — not disqualifying, since
`harvest_crossref.py` will recompute exact counts from the corrected prefix
at harvest time regardless.

**Method note**: Crossref's `query.container-title=...&rows=0` returns a
wildly inflated `total-results` (e.g. 232,848 "hits" for 2016) because the
relevance query doesn't restrict the count when combined with `rows=0` —
this is a known Crossref API quirk. The registry's original counts appear
to have been computed some other way (possibly already DOI-prefix based,
just with the wrong prefix string recorded afterward) since they're close
to today's true numbers despite citing a nonexistent prefix. Don't trust
`total-results` alone; always verify by sampling actual DOIs, as the skill
instructs.

### Volume trend, 2020–2025 (all modestly below `expected_per_year` [300,550])

2021 (249), 2022 (285), 2024 (278), 2025 (287) sit under 300 — 4–17% below
the lower bound, well inside the "~30% outside is worth a note" tolerance,
not the "2× outside → wrong source" red flag. This is very likely a real,
explainable trend rather than a counting problem: IMS's companion co-located
symposia have grown substantially over the same period and appear to be
absorbing content that used to run under the main IMS track — e.g. IWS
(International Wireless Symposium) grew from 86 papers in 2016 to 367 in
2025, becoming *larger than IMS itself* in 2024–2025. IMARC, IMBioC, WAMS,
IMOC all show similar growth. This reorganization culminates in the formal
2026 restructuring (see below), so the shrinking recent-year IMS-proper
counts read as the leading edge of that same trend, not noise or a wrong
source ID. Not proposing to change `expected_per_year` — flagging as
context, not a methodology change.

## Needs human review

1. **2026 scope decision (the main open item).** IEEE MTT-S restructured
   IMS2026 (Boston, June 7–12 2026) into a multi-symposium umbrella event.
   The direct technical continuation of "IMS" content is now split:
   - **IMS RFTT** ("Radio Frequency Technology and Techniques Symposium")
     — confirmed in Crossref, DOI prefix `10.1109/imsrftt43070.2026`, 160+
     papers so far (June 2026 conference, Crossref still indexing as of
     this report). Per IEEE MTT-S's own announcement, RFTT carries "much of
     the traditional IMS technical content... approximately half."
   - **IMS RFSA** ("Radio Frequency Systems and Applications") — the other
     ~half of former IMS content, per the same announcement. **Not yet
     found in Crossref** under that name; it appears to have absorbed IEEE
     RFIC (Radio Frequency Integrated Circuits Symposium), a conference
     that was historically separate and co-located with IMS, not previously
     counted under our `ims` key.

   I have not registered a 2026 entry in `years{}` — doing so would bake in
   a scoping guess. Options for the human to decide, none applied:
   (a) treat 2026 `ims` = RFTT only (undercounts vs. history, since RFTT is
   only ~half the old content); (b) treat 2026 `ims` = RFTT + RFSA combined
   (closer to historical scope, but RFSA isn't indexed yet and partly
   overlaps a conference — RFIC — never previously in this venue's scope);
   (c) treat 2026 as a hiatus/gap year for this key pending the field
   settling into its new structure, revisit in a future collection pass.
   This is a real editorial/methodology question (what counts as "the
   venue" post-split), not something I should resolve unilaterally.

2. **Registry `doi_prefix` errors, now corrected** (see table above) —
   2016–2019 cited a nonexistent `ims.YYYY` prefix (real: `mwsym.YYYY`);
   2022 and 2025 cited a wrong conference-ID number. These would have
   silently produced empty/wrong Crossref harvests for those years had they
   gone unnoticed. Recommend spot-checking other conference-key venues in
   the registry for the same class of error (wrong prefix string that
   happens to still carry a plausible-looking count).
