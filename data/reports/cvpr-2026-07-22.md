### On the 2016-2019 undershoot vs. `expected_per_year` floor (1500)

This is **real conference growth, not a filtering bug**. Checked two
specific risks the task called out:
- *Accidentally excluding valid main-track papers*: no — the Crossref query
  uses each year's exact main-track `container_title` (e.g. "2016 IEEE
  Conference on Computer Vision and Pattern Recognition (CVPR)") with no
  workshop wording, and the DOI-prefix spot-check (above) confirms the
  returned set is 100% genuine main-track CVPR DOIs.
- *Accidentally including/needing to exclude Workshops*: confirmed CVPRW is
  a structurally separate OpenAlex source (`S4363607748` for 2022, distinct
  `S4363608118`/`S4393918233` for the 2009 edition) and a structurally
  separate Crossref DOI-prefix family — sampled 5 works from
  `S4363607748` and all carry DOIs like `10.1109/cvprw56347.2022.*`, cleanly
  distinguishable from main-track `10.1109/cvpr52688.2022.*`-style prefixes.
  `openalex_ids` correctly contains only the main-track ID; no Workshops
  source is or was ever included.
- The counts themselves (652 → 793 → 992 → 1309 → 1482 → 1682 → 2083 → 2369
  → 2726 → 2877) form a smooth, monotonically increasing series with no dip
  or discontinuity that would indicate a query/filter regression in any
  single year — consistent with CVPR's well-documented growth in submission
  and acceptance volume over this decade. `expected_per_year` [1500, 3000] is
  a good fit for 2022-2025 but not the full 10-year window; that's a
  registry-authoring nuance (see "Needs human review"), not a data problem.
